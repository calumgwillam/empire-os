# EMPIRE OS — INITIAL ARCHITECTURE

## 1. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This document defines the initial structure of Empire OS.

It is intentionally Version 0.1.

The architecture must evolve through real operational use rather than assumptions.

---

## 3. CORE INFORMATION TYPES

Important organisational information should initially be classified into these broad types:

### Capture
Raw thought, note, observation or information that has not yet been evaluated.

### Opportunity
A potential source of strategic, financial or operational upside.

### Problem
Something currently reducing performance, quality, efficiency or reliability.

### Risk
Something that may cause future downside.

### Decision
A choice that has been made.

### Action
Something that must be done by a responsible person.

### Project
A coordinated body of work with a defined objective.

### Knowledge
Information worth retaining for future use.

### Lesson
Knowledge derived from experience, success or failure.

### Standard
An approved expectation, rule or method.

### System
A repeatable structure or process for producing an outcome.

### Metric
A quantitative measure of performance or condition.

### Evidence
Data, observations or records used to support understanding or decisions.

---

## 4. RELATIONSHIPS

Information should connect where useful.

Examples:

Capture → Opportunity

Capture → Problem

Problem → Investigation → Decision → Action

Problem → Decision → System Change → Lesson

Opportunity → Evaluation → Decision → Project → Outcome

Decision → Expected Outcome → Review → Actual Outcome

Task → Project → Strategic Objective → Business Pillar → Empire Objective

Employee → Role → Responsibility → Authority → Performance → Development

Customer → Property/Site → Job → Revenue → Feedback → Future Opportunity

Asset → Division → Operator → Maintenance → Cost → Utilisation

Metric → Target → Actual Result → Variance → Investigation

---

## 5. INITIAL OPERATING FLOW

Important information should generally move through a flow similar to:

Capture

→ Categorise

→ Evaluate

→ Decide

→ Assign

→ Execute

→ Review

→ Learn

→ Standardise

→ Automate where justified

Not every item must complete every stage.

---

## 6. EMPIRE / BUSINESS / EXECUTION LAYERS

### Empire Layer
Long-term direction, capital allocation, senior leadership, major risks, strategic opportunities and overall performance.

### Business Layer
Individual pillars or ventures, including their customers, people, finances, systems, projects and performance.

### Execution Layer
Day-to-day work, tasks, jobs, observations, issues, actions and field information.

The system should allow movement from high-level oversight into lower-level detail without mixing all information into one view.

---

## 7. FOUNDER ORCHESTRATION LAYER

Empire OS should eventually help identify:

- what needs founder attention
- what has materially changed
- what is off-course
- what decisions require founder authority
- what risks are increasing
- where leadership capability is weak
- where capital may be better allocated
- where opportunities deserve attention
- where founder involvement would create the greatest long-term value

The system should reduce unnecessary information overload.

---

## 8. ACCESS MODEL

The founder has total visibility.

Future access should be role-based.

Users should only see information relevant to their responsibilities, authority and legitimate operational needs.

Access design must support:

- confidentiality
- accountability
- clear authority
- information security
- operational usefulness

---

## 9. SYSTEM OF RECORD PRINCIPLE

Empire OS should become the trusted system of record for important organisational truth.

Important information should not be permanently dependent on:

- memory
- WhatsApp
- scattered notes
- individual inboxes
- verbal knowledge
- disconnected spreadsheets
- isolated AI conversations

External tools may still be used, but critical information should ultimately be represented inside Empire OS or linked to it reliably.

---

## 10. DEVELOPMENT CONSTRAINT

Do not build every possible category, dashboard or feature immediately.

The initial architecture exists to create a strong expandable foundation.

Real usage should reveal:

- what needs deeper structure
- what needs automation
- what belongs on dashboards
- what should remain simple
- what information is genuinely valuable
